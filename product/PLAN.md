# Buildlings: first revenue experiment

## Decision

Build a small consumer toy for people who spend time working with coding agents. Start with a useful emotional moment: waiting for a build feels a little less lonely, and finishing something gets a small celebration. This is a product hypothesis, not established demand.

The browser companion uses self-reported sessions. A transparent roaming Windows application is now implemented and released as a free preview. Verified agent events, a marketplace, random rewards, and token-use tracking remain outside this prototype.

## Customer and reason to buy

Target builders who already personalise their desktop, stream coding sessions, or share their projects. The free app has the whole basic loop. A buyer would purchase original appearance packs and artwork because they like the characters, not to access a productivity promise.

The hypothesis can fail: pets may be fun once and free customisation alternatives exist. The test must measure repeated use and purchases, not only compliments or views.

## First paid item

**After Hours companion pack — proposed price C$6, one-time.**

The prepared pack contains three companion colour schemes, matching SVG stickers, and a desktop wallpaper. Import its `after-hours.json` through “Add a colour pack.” It changes colours without bypassing free pet unlocks. Files work locally, are included in a progress backup, and require no additional subscription. There is no DRM; this is an art purchase supported by convenience and attachment to the characters.

The pack is prepared for review, not listed for sale. Its private delivery ZIP is kept outside the public site. Use screenshots of the actual pack in any listing. Do not promise more characters, lifetime updates, native integration, or a cross-device account.

Do not introduce paid random pulls or token-spend rewards in this experiment. Fixed contents make it easier for a buyer to judge the purchase.

## Distribution test

1. Publish the free prototype on a static HTTPS host after reviewing its appearance and behaviour.
2. Record a 15-second demonstration of the actual Windows pet roaming over an editor, jumping, and reacting to cursor strokes. Show the browser build celebration separately if relevant; the desktop pet does not yet have build integration.
3. The owner posts the demonstration in a few relevant communities where product sharing is permitted. Use existing personal accounts and follow each community's posting rules. No scraping, mass messaging, or automated outreach is planned.
4. Invite 20 early users to try it during a real coding session. Ask whether they kept it open, used it again the following day, and shared the image. These are proposed sample sizes, not current users.
5. If people use it again, review and list the finished pack through a digital-download checkout that supports the owner's Canadian seller account. Fulfilment should automatically deliver the ZIP. The owner completes account, payout, identity, and tax setup directly; credentials do not belong in source files or chat.
6. Aim for the first 10 actual pack purchases. At C$6 each that is C$60 gross, before payment/platform fees, taxes, or refunds. This is an experiment target, not a forecast.

Virality could help distribution, but does not itself establish a business. A video with many views and no repeat usage or buyers is weak evidence. A smaller group of repeat users who actually buy is more useful.

## Decision rules

- First check whether at least 5 of the first 20 testers voluntarily return on another day. This is an internal go/no-go heuristic, not an industry benchmark.
- If users like the character but stop keeping the native companion open, investigate the reason before adding species or social features.
- If there is repeat usage but no purchases, change the pack proposition once and test it. Do not interpret wish-list clicks as revenue.
- If users do not return, pause expansion and ask what they enjoyed and why they stopped. Avoid spending on ads to hide weak retention.
- Collect feedback directly during the small trial. This version has no analytics, shared profiles, public leaderboard, or mailing list.

## Current status

A public browser preview, tested Windows desktop release, and prepared browser cosmetic delivery pack exist. The desktop pet does not yet import that cosmetic pack. No customer acquisition, traffic, revenue, business registration, or live checkout has been verified. Buildlings is a working product name.
