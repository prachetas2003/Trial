# Sources for Mischief’s harder-moment quotations

These notes accompany H001–H035 in [MONKEY_QUOTE_BANK.md](MONKEY_QUOTE_BANK.md).
The speech bubble displays **only the quote and name**; these notes are editorial
references, not extra dialogue.

Checked on 11 October 2026 against the linked accessible copies of letters,
blog posts, speech transcripts, and nonfiction books. Several original websites
were inaccessible from the research environment; original publication references
and the copies actually read are distinguished below. Attribution here follows
those documents, including reported conversations where noted.

Whitespace and curly apostrophes are normalized for display. Excerpts retain
the source wording; exceptions concern PDF word spacing and excerpt boundaries,
as documented below. No invented motivational line is credited to a famous person.

## Warren Buffett

Original publication: [Berkshire Hathaway’s shareholder letter archive](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html), the 1985 and 2013 letters.

Copies read: [1985 text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ReeceHarding/buffett-letters/main/1985.md)
and [2013 text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ReeceHarding/buffett-letters/main/2013.md).
The mirror documents conversion from Berkshire’s originals.

- **H001** ends before the source’s colon and pregnancy example. Buffett is
  discussing patience in acquiring businesses, not a formula for personal productivity.
- **H009** comes from the investment principles section. The PDF conversion joins
  words in “whoseeyesaregluedtothescoreboard”; spaces have been restored for reading.
  The comparison between the playing field and scoreboard is retained verbatim.

## Mark Cuban

### Follow your effort

Original publication: [18 March 2012 blog post](https://blogmaverick.com/2012/03/18/dont-follow-your-passion-follow-your-effort/).

Copy read: [archived PDF of the post](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/op7ic/MCuban_Blog/main/posts/2012-03-18-DontFollowYourPassionFollowYourEffort.pdf).

- **H002** is the first sentence of the closing paragraph. “Passions” is plural in
  the original; the bank preserves that wording.
- **H010** is the first item of the post’s numbered list.
- **H017** is a complete sentence in the paragraph about time.

### Effort and results

Original publication: [30 December 2007 blog post](https://blogmaverick.com/2007/12/30/the-one-thing-in-life-you-can-control-effort/).

- **H023** is a complete sentence in Cuban’s comparison of business with sports.
- **H031** is a complete sentence in the paragraph distinguishing results from
  hours spent at work.

### Starting slowly

Original publication: [8 June 2004 blog post](https://blogmaverick.com/2004/06/08/rules-of-success-sweat-equity-is-the-best-equity/).

- **H027** preserves both consecutive sentences, including the original “OK” / “ok”
  capitalization. The surrounding discussion concerns financing and growing a business.

## Steve Jobs

Original publication: [Stanford’s commencement speech text](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says).

Copy read: [English speech text in a bilingual transcript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/haradats/Steve-Jobs-Stanford-Commencement-Speech-2005/master/Steve-Jobs-Stanford-Commencement-Speech-2005.tex).
The quotations were checked against the English paragraphs, not translated back
from the Japanese text.

- **H004, H019, H025** are from the story about being fired from Apple, returning
  to work he loved, and starting again.
- **H012, H029, H033** are from the final story about mortality and choosing one’s path.
- Each selection contains complete consecutive sentences or a complete sentence.
  “And” is retained in H025 and H033.

## Paul Graham

Original publication: [“How to Do Great Work”](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html).

Copy read: [Graham essays EPUB](https://github.com/ofou/graham-essays/releases/download/latest/graham.epub),
chapter “How to Do Great Work.” The collection’s [source index](https://github.com/ofou/graham-essays/releases/download/latest/essays.csv)
maps the chapter to the original essay. These release artifacts may be updated.

- **H003, H034** concern discovering a project by starting to work.
- **H011, H024, H032** concern getting started and working consistently.
- **H018, H028** concern beginning small and finishing projects.
- **H035** concerns interest as a reason to persist.
- All eight selections preserve complete source sentences; together they quote
  105 words of the essay.

## Charles Darwin

Publication reference: [Project Gutenberg edition of *The Autobiography of Charles Darwin*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2010),
edited by Francis Darwin; Charles Darwin wrote the autobiographical account in 1876.

Copy read: [GITenberg transcription of that edition](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GITenberg/The-Autobiography-of-Charles-Darwin_2010/master/2010.txt).

- **H007, H015** are complete sentences in the closing assessment of Darwin’s
  scientific habits. “These causes” in H015 refers to his love of natural science,
  ambition, and desire to understand what he observed.
- **H022** is the closing clause of the sentence about public approval and book
  sales in the account of the Beagle voyage. It retains the source’s final period.

## Thomas Edison

Publication reference: [Project Gutenberg edition of *Edison: His Life and Inventions*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/820)
by Frank Lewis Dyer and Thomas Commerford Martin, 1910.

Copy read: [GITenberg transcription](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GITenberg/Edison--His-Life-and-Inventions_820/master/820.txt),
chapter XXIV, “Edison’s Method in Inventing.” These are quotations attributed to
Edison within the biography, not quotations from its authors’ narration.

- **H006** is Edison’s reported reply in a conversation with Charles Batchelor and
  E. H. Johnson; the preceding interjection “Stuff!” is omitted.
- **H014** is Edison’s reply as recalled by Walter S. Mallory in the account of
  storage-battery experiments. It uses the documented “several thousand things”
  wording, rather than the widely circulated “10,000 ways” version.
- **H021** retains the historical wording “1 per cent.” and “99 per cent.”

## Richard Hamming

Original event: Bell Communications Research colloquium, 7 March 1986.
J. F. Kaiser edited the transcription; the transcript credits Donna Paradise
with the initial transcription from the recording.

Original transcript reference: [University of Virginia transcript](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html).

Copy read: [Matthew R. DeVerna’s LaTeX formatting of the English transcript](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mr-devs/hamming-you-and-your-research/main/main.tex),
which names the event, transcription credit, and original transcript reference.

- **H005** concerns having the courage to pursue important problems.
- **H013** concerns preparation alongside the role of luck.
- **H020** ends before the source’s dash and comparison with compound interest.
  It is Hamming’s own explanation; the earlier “Knowledge and productivity are
  like compound interest” quotation is credited to Hendrik Bode in the transcript
  and is not presented as Hamming’s original quote here.
- **H026** is a complete sentence from the discussion of believing a theory enough
  to work on it while remaining able to notice its flaws.
- **H030** is the closing exhortation before the transcript’s end-of-talk marker.
